My surname is **Gil de Avalle** (cite as Gil de Avalle, G.).

I am a PhD candidate in artificial intelligence at the [University of Groningen](https://www.rug.nl/), in the [OPERA research group](https://www.rug.nl/feb/research/operations/), specialising in grounded agentic AI for reliable industrial operations. My research addresses how agentic AI systems can be grounded in explicit domain knowledge so that the support they provide remains reliable in industrial settings where an incorrect instruction has real consequences.

Large language models produce fluent guidance without any commitment to the procedures an organisation actually sanctions. My work treats structured knowledge sources, and graph-based representations in particular, as the mechanism that constrains what an agent is permitted to say and do, and asks under which conditions structured traversal is preferable to unstructured retrieval. The setting is maintenance and operator support, where procedural knowledge is abundant but locked in diagrams and standard operating documents rather than in machine-readable form.

The work sits across three areas, and its aim lies where they meet: operator support that stays reliable and traceable, built on integrated knowledge.

My position is funded by AIXPERT, a Horizon Europe project on trustworthy AI for industrial operations, with Philips Drachten as the primary industrial partner. I am supervised by [Christos Emmanouilidis](https://www.rug.nl/staff/c.emmanouilidis/) and [Laura Maruster](https://www.rug.nl/staff/l.maruster/).

Previously, I worked as an AI Engineer and Consultant at Nexler, developing automation solutions using LLMs, APIs, and agentic frameworks, and as an Analytics Product Manager at BridgeU, developing predictive models for university admissions. I hold an MSc in Operations Management from the University of Groningen, graduated cum laude, with a thesis on machine learning applied to anomaly detection in wind turbines.

I am glad to hear from anyone working on knowledge graphs, retrieval grounding, or agentic decision support in industrial operations.
